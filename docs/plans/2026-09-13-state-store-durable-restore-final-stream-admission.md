# Restore stream admission amendment

2026-09-13. Frozen after revision 3 contract review. This records admission requirements, not passing runtime evidence.

The frozen restore contract requires one same-invocation final traversal of all selected receipts and source/current/output rows. The retained-source amendment also says every public-invocation storage operation uses a 4,096-operation ceiling. The independent routing review (`step3-restore-budget-routing-review.md`, SHA-256 `0341238be819a20254481f4942a91323bed6797db061b716cbcf9638680e4e32`) identifies the conflict: the required large final traversal can itself exceed 4,096 operations. This clarification classifies work explicitly; no counter is reset and no ordinary commit bound changes.

The existing cumulative WorkspaceIoBudget remains 32 MiB and 4,096 operations for the entire V8/mixed public invocation. It includes public request/attempt/journal/read-manifest records, snapshot/provider/required-object/pin/source-retention preflight, selected plan/progress/current/direct-predecessor metadata, lock and epoch claim/renew/refence/settlement/release, final seal/prepared descriptor, candidate HEAD publication, exceptional candidate recovery and all associated failures/retries/response ownership. Nested helpers and terminal-adoption reentry borrow this same budget. These records cannot be relabeled as stream data merely because they are large or repeated.

The standard merge unit independently admits at most 16 combined source/current leaves, 64 decodes, 64 MiB encoded input, 32 output blocks and a 32 MiB output packing target. Directory/index/descriptor boundary and lookahead work is included in its admitted selection, operations and ownership ledger; all physical operations are counted. Standard request peak remains at most 64 MiB with cache pools separate. Oversized singleton phases keep their frozen input and 64 MiB + 24P scratch ceilings and never retain both oversized values concurrently. This clarification does not admit extra input by starting a fresh unit within one public call.

Only after authenticated terminal progress selects final verification may the total-input final stream use its separate, monotonic work ledger. This phase is not another merge unit: it cannot select new source/current rows, reopen a cursor, change an ordinary-unit limit, or publish a candidate. The final stream reads only the predeclared classes below, and a record's class is fixed by its role and phase rather than its path, size, cache status, or number of retries.

* **Always control.** Snapshot/export, provider object, pin selector/revision, workspace request/attempt/journal/read-manifest, plan selector, current progress, direct predecessor, lock/epoch object, final seal, prepared descriptor, candidate HEAD and every associated HEAD/CAS/PUT/reconciliation response always charge the one `WorkspaceIoBudget`. They remain control even while final verification is running.
* **Final-stream inputs.** After terminal selection only, a forward immutable ordinal receipt page, the receipt records it names, and the exact immutable directory/index/descriptor/value blocks witnessed by those selected receipts are stream inputs. This is an explicit exception for the final traversal, not a dynamic relabeling rule. A mutable selector, progress object, journal, seal, prepared descriptor, candidate HEAD object, or any preflight object can never enter this class. A HEAD does not itself become a stream input. A HEAD or PUT that validates or writes an exact predeclared final-stream physical object is a final-stream operation and consumes that microchunk's operation and response-ownership admission. A HEAD, GET/range GET, PUT, or CAS on every Always-control record remains a WorkspaceIoBudget operation. No operation may choose its ledger from path size, cache state, retry count, or remaining budget. An ordinal receipt page remains individually limited to 4 MiB, is read from its exact authenticated path/digest witness, and is never accumulated or reversed in memory.
* **Final-stream outputs.** A page written by the final deterministic `directory::Builder` is stream output and retains the existing 64 KiB directory page limit. The final seal, prepared descriptor and candidate HEAD publication remain control writes.

Every final data object keeps its existing authenticated size/version/digest, range and decode ceilings: directory pages are at most 64 KiB; multirow production blocks are at most 256 KiB; a valid singleton segment remains within the existing 64 MiB segment ceiling. The standard 16-leaf/64-decode/64 MiB-selected-input/32-output-block/32 MiB-packed-output admission remains mandatory for every ordinary advance and cannot be expanded by preparing or entering final verification. Oversized singleton phases retain their separate frozen limits. This clarification permits total final traversal only; it does not permit a final chunk to hide an ordinary merge selection or a new oversized comparison.

Every final-stream read/write/HEAD and failed/repeated operation increments a final-stream counter before I/O, with bytes admitted before reads and returned HEAD/PUT ownership charged immediately after return. A final chunk must have a declared pre-I/O object, encoded-byte and operation reservation derived from the exact next ordinal receipt page and its authenticated descriptors. It releases raw inputs before the next chunk and holds no receipt-page collection, reverse chain, or persisted builder frontier. The final request peak is independently measured as the maximum of chunk scratch plus capacity-based carried state, not cumulative allocations across the whole stream. Counters report total final stream objects, bytes, operations, rows, decodes and hashes without pretending they fit an ordinary/unit request work ceiling.

`chunk_live_upper_bound` is exactly `chunk.cumulative_allocation_bytes + carried_owned_bytes_at_chunk_start`; its final reported bound is the maximum over chunks. The carried ledger records allocated capacity or explicit reservation, owner, creation/release chunk and byte count for builder levels, source/target/output cursors and lookahead, current/next receipt, rolling coverage/history, held plan/progress/source metadata, partial output/candidate buffers and request-owned copies. Cache-pool and backend-shared ownership are reported separately. The ledger must balance to zero on every terminal success and error path; unknown ownership, an unbalanced release, or a missing carry entry is nonpassing.

## Numeric admission, revision 3

Every standard final-stream microchunk has an independent hard ceiling of 64 MiB cumulative admitted range/PUT reservation bytes plus actual returned response-owned bytes and 4,096 physical operations, with at most 64 MiB total request-owned live memory INCLUDING all state carried into the microchunk. This is a new final-stream admission, not the 32 MiB workspace control ledger and not a change to ordinary merge-unit limits. The total final-stream ledger never resets. The microchunk ledger resets only after that exact bounded primitive finishes, its temporary ownership is released, and its complete totals are added to the final ledger; carried ownership is never reset or omitted. Admission includes errors, collisions, cache fallbacks and revalidation. Before an I/O or known allocation, charge its conservative reservation against the remaining microchunk allowance; returned HEAD/PUT capacities are charged immediately on arrival under the already frozen post-response rule, before another operation. Overflow/exhaustion fails closed. On return, charge `ObjectMeta` as `size_of::<ObjectMeta>() + path.capacity() + version.capacity() + etag.as_ref().map_or(0, String::capacity)` and charge `WriteResult` as `size_of::<WriteResult>() + returned_version.capacity()`. These charges count toward the same 64 MiB microchunk admission and live-ownership ledger before another operation. Response allocation is already returned when this check occurs; overflow or exhaustion fails closed and is nonpassing. No claim is made that the backend prevents allocation before returning those response objects.

Every `Bytes` returned by storage is classified at the adapter boundary as `new_request_owned`, `copied_request_owned`, `backend_shared`, or `unknown`. The first two charge their actual owned backing capacity to the current microchunk and enter carry if retained. `backend_shared` records held byte length and lifetime in the separate backend/cache report; a shared clone does not transfer that backing allocation to request ownership. `unknown` fails the microchunk after return and before the value can support final verification or another I/O. A cache clone uses the same explicit ownership classification; a copied allocation is request-owned. `Bytes::len()` alone is not evidence of owned backing capacity.

Before the first receipt-page read, authenticate the selected terminal `(next_ordinal, chain_digest, receipt_count)` from current progress under the control ledger. `next_ordinal` is the exclusive ordinal immediately after the final selected receipt, so the empty stream has ordinal and count zero. Initialize final-stream state only to the plan's exact genesis predecessor digest and expected ordinal zero. Each page and record must match the selected plan, attempt, owner generation, exact expected ordinal and running predecessor digest before it supplies a physical witness or Builder leaf. Completion requires that the next ordinal, receipt count and running digest equal the authenticated terminal tuple exactly. A missing, duplicate, reordered, substituted, or valid-looking strict prefix is failure and cannot mint the publication capability.

Microchunk boundaries are fixed algorithm steps, not arbitrary counter reset points:

- Decode and independently validate at most one ordinal receipt record, with its 4 MiB cap and fixed cap-plus-one probe. A HEAD used to admit a shorter immutable probe is an additional charged operation. The selected terminal chain digest/count is still checked at the end of the complete ascending traversal; reading an unanchored prefix does not confer publication authority.
- Traverse at most one source, current, or output directory path to its next leaf under an exact pinned root. Its existing page/fence-key/object limits still apply. The source-derived conservative path allowance (2,058 operations and 34,078,722 encoded probe bytes) fits the new numeric ceiling; actual carried/decoded allocations must independently fit the live ceiling. Do not combine all three directory paths in one cumulative-allocation peak sample.
- Authenticate at most one physical descriptor and its index, preserving the 1 MiB descriptor and 512 KiB index ceilings, exact role/root/leaf/version/size checks, and required pre/post HEAD validation. A physical cache fallback's extra reads and metadata remain charged; a seven-operation estimate is not a ceiling for a thirteen-operation fallback.
- Decode/hash/validate at most one normal multirow payload block of at most 256 KiB. Carry only bounded cursor/row comparison witnesses needed by the merge and history verifier; count their capacities. Payload bytes required for exact logical history must be streamed while owned or reread with a new charge, never reconstructed from a digest.
- Push at most one verified output leaf into the existing deterministic directory Builder, or finish its at-most-eight-level frontier once. Charge keys, pages, immutable PUT/collision readback and actual response ownership. Keep builder capacity in carried state. A persisted builder frontier remains outside scope.

An oversized singleton payload is a separate explicit microchunk: `P` is the authenticated encoded range length of exactly one source, current, or output singleton block in this phase, with `0 < P <= 64 MiB`. The payload microchunk admits at most 4,096 operations, no second payload range, no second payload in carry, and request-owned live scratch at most 64 MiB + 24P INCLUDING carry. Descriptor/index/directory metadata and publication control work remain separate bounded steps. Rereads are charged, no two oversized payloads are retained together, and a logical cursor cannot advance until every required comparison/output phase finishes. This does not relax the frozen standard-unit 64 MiB selected-input cap or permit two oversized inputs by splitting one ordinary unit invisibly.

These numeric ceilings are admission requirements to implement and verify, not measured green results. A proposed primitive whose conservative scratch plus carry cannot fit must split at one of the fixed steps or return typed backpressure before the known allocation. Existing helpers with hidden I/O, unchecked response capacities, undefined Bytes backing ownership or missing builder-capacity accounting cannot be used unchanged and claimed passing. Large-fixture proof, fallback/fault accounting, exact carried-state measurement and the independent final-source audit remain mandatory.

Final stream iteration must periodically renew the held coordination lease and check the original source and execution deadlines, selected plan ownership and writer/reclamation fences. Those checks remain control-budget operations. Exhausted control admission, elapsed deadline, failed renewal, cancellation or corrupt stream evidence stops finalization without minting the private publication capability. Any possibly existing prepared candidate switches restart to read-only exact-candidate reconciliation. No resumed final pass resets the original request deadline or uses a persisted seal as publication authority.

The evidence must expose control and stream ledgers separately and show their sum for all physical operations. Missing/overlapping classification, unowned buffers or uncounted retries are nonpassing. Include preventing tests that (1) reject an ordinary unit over any frozen selection/byte/decode/output cap before I/O even when final verification is pending, (2) reject a journal/selector/seal/HEAD operation incorrectly offered as a stream input, (3) show a full final stream above 4,096 *stream* operations while the unchanged control ledger remains below its ceiling, (4) exhaust control admission during a long stream and prevent seal/prepared/HEAD publication, and (5) reject a final chunk whose declared reservation exceeds the newly frozen chunk bound before I/O. Large fixture construction remains separate from executed restore work.

This amendment makes the already required total-input final pass implementable under explicit resource accounting. It is not a large-restore result, a relaxation of ordinary catalog work, or authority to omit final verification.
